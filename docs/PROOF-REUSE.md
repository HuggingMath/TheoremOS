# Reuse an archived proof in a new problem

[简体中文](PROOF-REUSE.zh-CN.md) · [TheoremOS](../README.md)

A TheoremOS maintainer initiated these reuse trials using the public archive. Codex wrote the Lean files for the new statements and ran the checks with the recorded Lean and Mathlib versions. The examples can be checked locally without a TheoremOS account or model key.

- **Spectral theory.** Apply the finite-square lemma from the Anderson proof to a deterministic step potential on the square lattice. When the two spectral bands touch or overlap, they give the full spectrum. [Step potential example](../examples/anderson-step-potential/README.md)
- **Algebraic number theory.** Turn Dimitrov's bound into a threshold that depends only on the field degree. If every conjugate of a nonzero algebraic integer has modulus strictly below that threshold, the integer is a root of unity. [Roots of unity example](../examples/dimitrov-degree-bound/README.md)
- The **combinatorial optimization** example uses the triangle expansion theorem to transfer a budget and an objective on the original edges to the corresponding face of the expanded matching polytope. The attainable costs agree for every budget. [Budgeted matching example](../examples/triangle-weighted-matching/README.md)
- The **real analysis** example uses a lemma from the Hopf–Rinow proof. If the weak derivative on an interval is represented by a continuous function on the real line, its integral recovers the locally integrable function up to a constant and equality almost everywhere. For a continuous function, the integral between any two interior points gives its increment. [Weak fundamental theorem example](../examples/weak-fundamental-theorem/README.md)
- The **distance geometry** example uses a Gram realization lemma from the equiangular lines archive to decide whether a table of squared distances can be realized in a given dimension. It shows that a 3–4–5 triangle needs at least two dimensions. [Euclidean distance table](../examples/distance-geometry/README.md)
- The **coding theory** example uses cancellation of row ratios in a six-phase Hadamard matrix whose entrywise square is also Hadamard. Distinct binary projection words have Hamming distance at least three, so a received word within one bit of a projected word determines it uniquely. [Six-phase code](../examples/six-phase-code/README.md)
- The **automata theory** example uses a congruence lemma to check whether proposed state merges preserve a language. It shows how merging a pair of states in a four-state parity machine yields a two-state machine for the same language. [State merging](../examples/dfa-state-merging/README.md)
- The **statistical inference** example applies the tree reconstruction bound to classification with equally likely classes. When at most one class has likelihood above the mixture at each observation, the difference between two observation distributions gives a lower bound on optimal accuracy. A three-class experiment with two possible observations attains the bound. [Multiclass hypothesis testing](../examples/multiclass-hypothesis-testing/README.md)

## One lemma used in two archived proofs

Two three-state tree reconstruction proofs call `pmfTV_le_card_mul_reconAdvantage`. If the leaf observation laws under two root states differ by at least $\delta$ in total variation, the lemma gives reconstruction advantage at least $\delta/3$. The two records contain byte-identical copies of the lemma's source file. Both record a local comparator exit code of `0`.
