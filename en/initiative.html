THEOREMOS / The initiativeFor mathematical research

Building a researchworkbench together

An invitation to mathematicians, Lean formalizers and tool builders to shape how we move from a problem to proof attempts and a checked result.

TheoremOS is in allowlist beta. Read the public proof archive, then bring a concrete problem or idea.

01Start with everyday mathematical work

How should a problem be stated? Which assumptions must survive formalization? What should a failed attempt leave behind? These details determine whether a tool is useful in everyday research. We want to understand your workflow and the steps your current tools make difficult.

Bring a public problem, an unfinished Lean draft, a dependency or toolchain requirement, or a usage issue that is hard to reproduce. One concrete case can give us a place to begin.

Explore the current research entry points

02Keep proofs with the evidence for them

A result that others can inspect needs an explicit problem, a source version and a checking environment. Matching a natural-language statement to Lean requires reviewing its assumptions, quantifiers and goals. Lean checks of that formal statement are recorded separately.

Help improve statement review, diagnostics and the way evidence is read. The public archive preserves original attribution and distinguishes official acceptance, local verification and work still in progress.

Read a verification record

03Give methods and lemmas a longer life

Auxiliary lemmas, dependencies and failed approaches from one investigation can inform the next. Reuse should make a lemma’s type, assumptions and source visible, with a fresh check in the new environment.

Share Mathlib experience, ways of organizing public lemmas, search and context suggestions, or evaluation cases with a fixed environment. When comparing strategies, we can specify the tasks, comparison settings and acceptance conditions together.

Explore the current reuse workflow

04Keep research choices with the researcher

Researchers choose the problem, formal goals, model connection and what to make public. Your problems, source and proofs remain yours. You choose whether to publish a research record.

Participation can begin with feedback, an evaluation note or a collaboration need. We can discuss the form of any code, material or research contribution by email. The public repository currently contains the website, documentation and selected proof results.

Visit the public repository
